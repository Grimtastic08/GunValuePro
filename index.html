Gun Store Counter Handbook: NRA Condition Standards & Valuation Rules

Standardized criteria for inspecting walk-in firearms, avoiding wholesale anchor traps, and protecting store profit margins.

The "Wholesale Ceiling" Golden Rule

Never price a standard used firearm at or above your distributor wholesale cost for a new one. If Lipsey's or RSR will sell you a factory-new Glock 19 Gen 5 with two fresh magazines, lock, warranty, and factory box for $475, a customer walking through your front door will rarely pay $499 for a used one when they can get a brand-new one for $539.

1. The New Alternative Anchor If a used gun in your display case isn't at least 15-20% cheaper than your new inventory price, it sits in the case gathering dust.
2. Inventory Turn Velocity Fast-turning carry guns (G19, P365) can be bought at 68-70% of used retail. Slow or niche calibers must be bought at 55-60% to cushion capital tie-up.
3. The Trade-In Multiplier Offering 10-15% more on store credit captures the retail profit margin on the new gun or ammunition the customer purchases with that credit.

NRA Modern Firearm Condition Standards

New in Box (NIB) / 100% 1.02x - 1.05x

Pristine factory condition, completely unfired outside proof tests. Must include all factory accessories, matching serialized outer box, manuals, cable lock, and factory test target/envelope if applicable.

Counter Check: Look for powder burns in chamber/recoil lugs and brass marks on bolt face. If fired, immediately downgrade to Excellent.
Excellent / 95% - 98% 0.92x - 0.95x

Near new condition; used but very little. No noticeable finish wear except microscopic handling marks or light friction on barrel hood / slide rails. Bore mirror bright, stock/grips without dings or scratches.

Counter Check: Check muzzle crown for holster rubbing and edges of slide/frame.
Very Good / 85% - 90% (Industry Standard Used) 0.82x - 0.85x

Perfect working condition, no appreciable wear on working surfaces. No corrosion or pitting, only minor surface scratches, edge wear, or light holster wear on high contact points. Clean bore with sharp rifling.

Counter Check: Most honest daily carry guns and well-maintained hunting rifles fall here.
Good / 75% - 80% 0.70x - 0.75x

Safe working condition, more wear on working surfaces than "Very Good". Minor surface pitting that does not interfere with function, stock scratches, dings, bluing worn at grip and carrying points. Bore clean without heavy erosion.

Counter Check: Check lockup on revolvers and bolt play on rifles. Must cycle smoothly.
Fair / 60% - 70% 0.55x - 0.60x

Safe working condition but well worn. May require minor maintenance or replacement of non-critical springs. Noticeable bluing loss (40-60%), prominent scratches, stock cracks that don't affect safety, minor bore frost.

Counter Check: Factor in gunsmith cleaning and test-firing costs before making an offer.
Poor / < 60% (Parts / Project Gun) 0.35x - 0.40x

Extensive wear, severe rust or deep pitting, missing parts or broken safety mechanisms. Unsafe to shoot in current condition without major gunsmith restoration.

Counter Check: Buy primarily as wall-hangers, parts donors, or pass entirely.

Physical Counter Inspection Routine

1. Chamber Clear & Bore Drop magazine, lock slide open, visually inspect chamber. Use bore light to check rifling lands, crown burrs, and corrosion.
2. Lockup & Action On revolvers: check cylinder gap and cylinder timing. On semi-autos: check slide-to-frame fit, barrel hood lockup, and disconnector.
3. Trigger & Safeties Test thumb safety, grip safety, and firing pin block. Verify the trigger hasn't been unsafely lightened by an amateur kitchen-table gunsmith.
4. Serial Verification Match frame serial number with slide and barrel (especially on Glocks, SIGs, and European imports) for matching numbers.
